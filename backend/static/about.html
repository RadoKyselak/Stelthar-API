About Project Mirador

A free fact checker that answers from official U.S. government data.

What it does

Give Project Mirador a claim, such as “Unemployment in Texas was 4.1% in 2023”. It looks the figure up in the agency’s own data, tells you whether the claim holds, and links the exact source so you can read it yourself. You can also ask for a number directly and get it with its citation.

How to use it

  • In Chrome: add the extension, highlight any sentence on a page, right-click and choose Check with Project Mirador. The answer opens in the side panel.
  • On the web: type a claim or a question on the home page.

Where the answers come from

From the agencies’ public data services, not from web pages or other people’s summaries.

  • Jobs and prices: Bureau of Labor Statistics (unemployment, inflation (CPI), wages, jobs, by state or nationally).
  • The economy: Bureau of Economic Analysis (GDP, personal income, trade, government receipts and spending).
  • People and places: Census Bureau (population, income, poverty, home values, education, health insurance).
  • Federal money: Treasury and USAspending.gov (the national debt, agency budgets and spending by year).
  • Laws and votes: Congress.gov and state legislatures (whether a bill passed, the recorded vote, who sponsored it, when it was signed).
  • Crime: FBI Crime Data Explorer (violent crime, property crime and murder, by state or nationally).
  • Health: CDC and FDA (life expectancy, deaths from COVID-19, overdoses, firearms and other causes, drug approvals, labels and recalls).
  • Consumers and housing: FTC and HUD (fraud losses, FTC rules, area median income and income limits).
  • Food: USDA FoodData Central (calories and nutrients in a food).
  • Other countries: World Bank (population, GDP, unemployment and more for one country).

Questions

Where do the answers come from?

From the agencies' own public data services, not from web pages: the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Census Bureau, the Treasury, USAspending.gov, Congress.gov, the FBI, the CDC, the FDA, the FTC, HUD, the USDA and, for other countries, the World Bank.

What do the verdicts mean?

Supported: the record matches the claim. Mostly accurate: the claim is off by a small step in its last digit. Misleading: a real figure is used in a way the record does not support. Contradicted: the record says otherwise. No verdict: no official record covers the claim, and the answer says why.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It is free and there are no accounts, ads or trackers. The only thing sent is the text you choose to check.

Can the extension read the pages I visit?

No. It only receives the text you highlight and choose to check, or type in yourself.

Is Project Mirador a government service?

No. It is an independent project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by any agency whose data it shows.
