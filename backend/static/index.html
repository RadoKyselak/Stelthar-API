How a claim is checked

  1. 1

    Read the claim

    Mirador pulls out the figure, what it measures, the period and the place, plus how precisely the figure was written, so “9%” and “9.06%” are held to different standards.

  2. 2

    Fetch the official figure

    The number comes straight from the agency’s own data service. The period and place are read from the agency’s response, never assumed.

  3. 3

    Compare, and show the work

    You get the verdict, both numbers side by side, the size of any gap, and a link to the exact series. Nothing is hidden behind a score.

The rules it follows

  • No verdict without a source. Every answer links to the published data or document it rests on. If no source can be found, it says so.
  • Precision is respected. “9%” matches 9.06%. “4.1%” against an official 4.0% is Mostly accurate, not true and not false.
  • Revisions are not your error. A claim that matches an agency’s first published estimate is supported, with a note on the later revision.
  • “Can’t check” is never dressed up as a verdict. An outage, a missing period or an unsupported place each get their own plain explanation.
  • AI is the last resort. Exact-figure checks use no AI at all. When a claim needs a web search, the model may only report what a source says, under strict daily limits.
