For developers

A free JSON API. No key, no account. Last updated November 17, 2026.

What you get

The same checks the extension and this site run. Send a claim or a passage; get back a verdict, the official figure, the agency series it came from and a ready-made citation. Base address: https://factcheckgov.app.

Check a passage

POST /v2/check takes up to 8,000 characters and answers each claim in it.

curl -s https://factcheckgov.app/v2/check \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"text": "Inflation hit 9.1% in June 2022. Gas averaged $7 a gallon in June 2022."}'
  • claims: one entry per claim, in order. span is the claim as written, claim is what was checked, result is the answer (the same object /v2/verify returns).
  • tally: how many claims fell under each verdict.
  • deeper: true when agency data did not settle a claim and the full check might. Send that claim to /v2/verify.
  • "mode": "page" keeps only the claims agency data can settle.
  • "said_on": "2012-02-08" is the day the text was said. A claim that names no period (“unemployment is 8.3%”) is then held to the figure that was newest that day. If it does not match, there is no verdict: the claim may be about another period.
  • {"url": "https://..."} in place of text fetches that public page and checks it as a page; the answer adds page (address, site, title, and published, the day the page says it came out). A month named without a year is read against that day, and inherited on the claim says so. Sites that refuse automated readers answer 400 with the reason. Video and social media pages are not read.

One claim in the text gets the full check. Two or more are checked on agency data only.

Check one claim

POST /v2/verify takes {"claim": "..."}, up to 1,000 characters.

curl -s https://factcheckgov.app/v2/verify \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"claim": "Unemployment in Texas was 4.1% in 2023"}'
  • verdict: Supported, Mostly accurate, Contradicted, Misleading or No verdict.
  • confidence: High, Medium or Low.
  • reason: set when there is no verdict; says what would have to change for one to exist.
  • evidence: the official figure, its period, place, agency, series and source address.
  • revision: set when the claim matches a figure the agency has since revised.
  • tier and model_calls: how the answer was reached. Most are structured with zero model calls.
  • display: the same answer in plain language, ready to show: the label, a summary, how the claim was read, the sources and a citation.

Look up a figure

POST /v2/lookup takes {"query": "unemployment rate texas 2023"} and returns the figure with its citation in several forms. On a miss, suggestions names what it can fetch.

For assistants

POST /mcp speaks the Model Context Protocol (streamable HTTP), so an assistant that supports it can check claims and look up figures as tools. It offers check_text, check_page, check_claim and find_figure, all answered from agency data only.

Limits and fair use

  • Per connection: 20 claim checks a minute and 300 a day; 6 passage checks a minute and 60 a day; 60 lookups a minute and 2,000 a day. Over a limit, the answer is 429 with Retry-After.
  • Calls from a web page in someone’s browser are not allowed (no cross-origin access). Call from your server.
  • Answers come from agencies that revise their figures. Store the date with anything you keep.
  • The service is free and comes with no guarantee of uptime. If you build something that depends on it, say hello first.
